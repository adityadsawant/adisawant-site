Research

AI-driven operational intelligence for quality and manufacturing systems in deep-tech hardware startups.

Doctoral research

PHD CANDIDATE · INFORMATION SYSTEMS ENGINEERING AND MANAGEMENT · HARRISBURG UNIVERSITY OF SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY

My doctoral research investigates how deep-tech hardware startups — companies developing genuinely novel physical products without an established manufacturing history to draw on — can build AI-driven operational intelligence into their quality and manufacturing systems. Legacy industries like automotive or aerospace inherit decades of process capability data, failure libraries, and supplier quality history. Deep-tech hardware startups, by contrast, are often building the failure library in real time, alongside the product itself.

This work extends the same question I've been sitting inside of professionally: how emerging hardware companies can build data-driven quality operations without the manufacturing maturity that legacy industries take for granted.

Published paper

REVIEW PAPER · INTERNATIONAL JOURNAL OF SCIENCE AND RESEARCH (IJSR) · VOL. 15, ISSUE 9 · SEPTEMBER 2026

Artificial Intelligence in Manufacturing Quality: Applications, Challenges, and Emerging Research Directions

Aditya Sawant, Pratima Shinde · pp. 1659–1667

A review of peer-reviewed studies from January 2021 to September 2026 on how AI supports manufacturing quality, organized by quality function rather than by algorithm — from automated inspection and predictive quality to process monitoring, explainable diagnosis, quality knowledge management, and integrated decision support — along with the open challenges around data quality, model drift, explainability, and industrial validation.

Research interests

Beyond the dissertation, my broader research interest sits at the boundary between classical reliability engineering and emerging tooling — where methods like Weibull analysis, HALT/HASS, and DOE meet AI-assisted diagnosis, log parsing, and pattern detection in hardware quality data. Two areas I'm following closely:

  • IC design and VLSI — as a longer-term learning direction, deepening the connection between quality engineering and the semiconductor layer underneath the systems I test.
  • Applied scripting for QE workflows — using bash and lightweight tooling for log parsing, hardware diagnostics, and network debugging, in service of faster root-cause work.
